# Project status

What EasyEdit can do in its current release, and what is planned next. For roles, formats and limits, see [reference.md](reference.md).

## What works today

- Sign in with Google. Sign-up is open to anyone with a Google account, and everyone gets a Personal space with its own Google Drive storage, alongside any teams they join. While the server leaves sign-up closed, anyone not on the allowlist or invited to a team is turned away.
- A short welcome for new accounts, on their first sign-in: connect the Google Drive of the account you signed in with, or choose a different Google account, for your Personal space's videos (shown only when Personal spaces and storage are set up; whether it worked or not, you come back to this step and it says so before you move on), then connect an AI agent. Every step can be skipped. Someone who joins a team from an invite sees only the AI agents step, then lands in the team. Accounts that existed before it was added skip it.
- An Admin page where admins add people, change their role, and remove them.
- A dashboard where editors create projects in one of five video formats or at a custom size (in pixels, centimeters or inches), keep them in folders, and rename, duplicate and delete them. Recent shows the projects you opened last. A deleted project is hidden and not shown again. Each card shows a picture of its project, which the editor draws a few seconds after a change is saved; a project changed from outside the editor shows its new picture once an editor opens it.
- The editor, which opens a project in the whole window: an add panel on the left, the preview in the middle, the details on the right and the timeline along the bottom. It is always dark, and it asks the Dark Reader browser extension to leave it alone. In it you can:
  - add text clips from three presets (Title, Subtitle, Lower third) and video, image and audio clips from the Media tab, and move, trim, split, delete, ripple-delete, copy and paste clips of every kind
  - add, rename, lock, hide, mute, reorder and remove tracks
  - play the project in the preview, with frame steps, J, K and L, and safe-zone guides for each format
  - undo and redo your own changes, and undo any change listed under Activity, including a teammate's
- Export: the **Export** button (or `Cmd/Ctrl+E`) makes an MP4 of the project on your own computer, in the browser; nothing is uploaded. You pick a size, watch the progress and can cancel, and you can keep editing while it runs. Video and image clips export from the files the editor plays, so video comes from its 720p preview copy. The sound is mixed as the preview plays it: each video's own sound and the audio tracks, at each clip's volume (up to 1), with muted tracks and hidden video tracks silent; a clip at another speed keeps its pitch. Without an AAC encoder (Firefox, Chrome on Linux) the file has no sound track; when the project has sound, the dialog says so before you export. Viewers can export too. It needs a recent Chrome or Edge on a computer.
- Upload video, images and audio in the editor's Media tab (up to 2 GB a file). Uploads go straight from your browser to the team's Google Shared Drive and resume after an interruption.
- Put media on the timeline, trim it, and play it in the preview. Each video gets a small 720p preview copy, so phone footage plays on any laptop.
- Every change is saved as you make it. Other people's changes show up within about two seconds while you are working, and the top bar says who is editing.
- Viewers get the same editor, read-only: they can play and look, but not change anything.
- A project API (see [The project API](api.md)).
- Each space has a brand kit: fonts for titles, subtitles and body text (about 40 fonts, Orbitron and Inter among them), a color palette and logos. It is in the editor's **Brand kit** tab, where a click adds the text, recolors the selected text or adds the logo, and on the dashboard under the spaces. New text from the Text tab or an AI agent starts in the kit's fonts, and the inspector's **Font** menu lists the kit's fonts first.
- A welcome dialog with a short narrated intro video (spoken voice and captions), shown once to each person on their first visit to their dashboard, and a **Learn** page where it can be watched again at full size (the **Learn** link at the top of the dashboard, or **Tutorial** in the account menu), on servers that have the video ([Tutorial video](setup.md#tutorial-video)).
- Claude, ChatGPT and other AI agents can list, read, create and edit projects for a signed-in member, through the connector (set up on the AI agents page). When you have more than one space, an agent asks which space a new project goes in. They can also list the team's media and put it on a timeline. A member can connect several agents at once.
- Effects: the right panel's **Effects** tab, beside Inspect and Activity, holds the selected clip's effects. Video and image clips take a transition in and out (fade, crossfade from the clip before, slide, zoom, whip or dip to a color), with its length and direction. Keyframes animate a clip's position, scale, rotation and opacity, each with its own easing: the diamond buttons beside Inspect's fields add them, and the Effects tab lists every keyframe with its time, value and easing to edit, **Go to** and **Delete** on each and **Clear** for a property, and **Ken Burns** for a slow push in across an image. Text clips animate in and out (fade, pop, slide, and typewriter or scale punch on the way in) and can shake, pulse or highlight their words in a color for the whole clip. The timeline marks transitions and keyframes on each clip, the export draws them as the preview does, and AI agents can read and set them.
- Templates: a template is a project with slots, the empty places for text, video, images or audio that get filled in later. Each space keeps its own templates, and three starters (Match Recap, Robot Reveal and Season Recap) are there from the start. In the editor, **Make this a slot** in the inspector marks a clip as a slot, and **Save as template** keeps the project as a template of the space (a template with the same name can be replaced). A new project can start from a template on the dashboard, and the **Templates** page in space settings lists them and edits or deletes the saved ones. An empty slot shows as a placeholder; drop a video, image or audio file from Media onto it, or select it and click the file, to fill it. An empty slot exports as a black or silent gap, and the export dialog names the slots still empty. Media that a template keeps cannot be deleted until the template is. AI agents can list and read templates, start a project from one and fill its slots.
- Daily limits keep one person from filling the storage or the database by accident. Over any 24 hours, each person can save 20 MB of changes (at up to 600 changes a minute), create 25 new projects (a duplicate counts as one) and start 500 uploads (resuming an unfinished upload does not count). Each space can hold up to 200 folders. AI agents have their own separate allowance, so an agent does not use up yours. When you reach a limit, the app says so and keeps what you were doing open; nothing already saved is affected, and it saves again once the limit allows.
- Linked clips: `Cmd/Ctrl+G` links the selected clips and `Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+G` unlinks them. A click on a linked clip in the timeline selects its whole group, so one drag moves them all and one Delete removes them all; hold `Alt` to take the one clip, and a click on the preview still selects one. Linked clips show a small chain mark. **Separate audio** leaves the video and its new audio clip linked.
- Export a part: the Export dialog's **What to export** menu exports the whole project, only the span of the selected clips, or from the playhead to the end. A clip cut at the edge of the part keeps the same picture and sound on every frame it still has.
- Export quality: the Export dialog's **Quality** menu gives the picture fewer bits for a smaller file, the standard amount, or more for footage with a lot of motion or detail. The size it may reach is shown before you export.
- Frame rate: a new blank project is made at 24, 25, 30, 50 or 60 frames a second, picked in the New project dialog (30 unless you pick another). A project keeps the rate it was made with, and a project from a template has the template's. The preview, the timeline and the export all run at the project's rate.
- Full quality: the Export dialog has a **Full quality** switch, off unless you turn it on, wherever a video plays from a smaller copy of itself. With it on, each video's picture is drawn from its original file and not the 720p copy the editor plays; the originals are downloaded first, so it is slower. A file this browser can't read, or a high dynamic range one, exports from its copy as before. Sound still comes from the file the editor plays.
- Sound clean-up: a video or audio clip's Sound section has **Clean up voice**, which in one change takes out rumble, turns down the gaps between words, lifts speech where it is understood and brings loud and quiet words closer together; **Reset** takes it off. Each part can be set on its own: **Cut rumble**, **Noise gate**, **Compressor**, and **Bass**, **Presence** and **Treble** in decibels. With several clips selected the button cleans up each one. It is heard in the preview and is in the export, from the same code (`src/lib/audio/clean-dsp.ts`; the preview runs it in an audio worklet, `public/audio/clean-worklet.js`, made by `node scripts/make-worklet.mjs`). In a browser without audio worklets the preview plays clips as recorded; the export is cleaned all the same. It needs no model and no service. Not built: noise reduction that removes hiss under speech, and studio-grade voice repair.
- Limiter: the whole mix is held just under full scale (−1 dB), in the preview and in every export, so a clip turned up past 100% or several loud clips together are turned down smoothly where they used to be clipped flat. A mix that never gets that loud is not changed at all.
- Mixing: selecting a video, overlay or audio track shows its **Track volume**, which scales every clip on it on top of each clip's own volume. A video or audio clip's **Pan** moves its sound towards the left or the right. The preview and the export follow both.
- Voiceover: **Record voiceover** in the Media tab records your microphone while the project plays from the playhead. **Stop recording** saves the take as a sound file, uploads it like any other file, and puts it on an audio track where the recording began. The browser asks for the microphone the first time; wear headphones so the project's own sound is not recorded with your voice. **Record screen** and **Record camera** beside it record a window or tab, or your camera with your microphone, and land the take on a video track the same way.
- Green screen and rounded corners: in a video or image clip's **Color** section, **Green screen** takes one color out of the picture so what is under the clip shows there, with the color, how near it a pixel must be and how softly the edge fades; **Corner rounding** rounds the picture's corners, up to a circle for a square crop. Both show in the preview and the export.
- Subtitle files: the **Captions** tab downloads the project's caption lines as a `.srt` or `.vtt` file, timed on the project's own clock, to post beside the exported video. **Import subtitles** reads a `.srt` or `.vtt` file as the captions of the one selected video or audio clip: each line lands where the clip plays at that moment, and a line's words share its time evenly.
- Trim modes: the button beside **Snap** (or `W`) sets what dragging a clip's edge does. **Trim** is the plain one. **Ripple trim** moves every later clip on the track with the edge, so no gap opens and nothing is covered; at a clip's start the clip stays where it is and gains or loses footage at its head. **Roll** moves the cut between two clips that touch, one growing as the other shrinks. A video or audio clip's **Footage from** field in the inspector slips it: it starts from another moment of its file and keeps its place and length.
- Markers: **B** puts a marker at the playhead, or takes away the one there. Markers show as flags on the timeline's ruler, are kept with the project for everyone, and can be named and removed in the inspector when nothing is selected. `Alt+Up` and `Alt+Down` jump between them, and dragged clips, the Razor and the playhead's jumps stop at them as they do at clip edges.
- Cut by the words: once a clip has captions, the **Captions** tab's lines have a second mode, **Cut video**. Each word is a button; pick words and **Cut words** takes those moments out of the video itself and moves up what follows on the clip's track. **Cut pauses** removes every silence between words longer than 0.6 seconds, leaving a breath either side, and **Cut filler words** removes the ums and uhs. Each is one change and one undo, and says how much shorter the video got. It cuts only the clip's own track, so music or titles on other tracks stay where they are.
- Volume over time: the ◆ beside **Volume** keys a video or audio clip's volume at the playhead, so it can rise and fall across the clip; the keyframes are listed and edited in the Effects tab like any others, and the preview and the export follow them. **Duck under speech** (`Shift+D`, or the button on an audio clip) turns the selected music down to a quarter while other clips are heard over it and back up after; where a file has captions it follows the words, so the music returns in a long pause.
- Color: a video or image clip's **Color** section in the inspector has ready-made looks (Vivid, Warm, Cool, B&W, Fade, Vintage, Drama) and the numbers behind them: brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, hue, vignette and blur. **Reset color** takes the picture back to as recorded. **Blend** sets how a video, image, text or shape mixes with what is under it (Multiply, Screen, Overlay and others), and flips a video or image sideways or upside down. All of it shows in the preview, in thumbnails and in the export, and AI agents can read and set it. A browser that cannot filter a canvas (older Safari) shows the picture without its colour changes; the export, which needs Chrome or Edge, always has them.
- Speed ramps: the **Speed** section of a video or audio clip that plays forward has **Speed ramp** (Speed up, Slow down, Hero, Bullet, Flash in, Flash out) and **Apply ramp**. The clip becomes up to ten short linked pieces, each a little faster or slower than the one before, playing its footage end to end; what follows on the track moves to fit. One undo puts the clip back.
- Scopes: in the **Color** section, **Scopes** shows the frame in the preview as a histogram, a waveform or a vectorscope, redrawn as the preview is (while a wheel or a curve is dragged too). They are counted only while shown.
- Masks: a video, image or adjustment layer has a **Mask** section in the inspector. Switched on, only a rectangle or ellipse of the picture shows (or, with **Invert**, everything but it), placed and sized in percent of the picture, with a **Soft edge**. On an adjustment layer the mask is the part of the frame the layer changes, which is how to blur, grey or pixelate one region. It is drawn the same in the preview, thumbnails and the export.
- Slide: the trim-mode button in the control row (and `W`) now steps through four modes. With **Slide** on, dragging a clip that touches the clips beside it moves it between them: the one before ends later or sooner and the one after starts later or sooner, so the clip keeps its footage and length and nothing else on the track moves. It stops where a neighbour would run out of footage or of frames.
- Keyframe curves and crop keyframes: in the **Effects** tab a keyframe's Ease menu has **Custom curve**, which shows the travel to the next keyframe as a curve with two handles to drag (or nudge with the arrow keys); a handle pulled past the top or bottom overshoots and comes back. Each side of a clip's crop has a keyframe diamond in the inspector, so a crop can open, close or wipe across over time; **Reset crop** clears them.
- Media in a folder on your computer: in the **Media** tab, **Use a folder on this computer** asks for a folder (Chrome and Edge). From then on, files you add are copied into that folder and not sent to Google Drive, with the preview copy and still picture made from each beside them, and the editor plays them from there. It works in a space that has no storage connected at all. The library still lists every file for the whole space, so teammates and your other devices see the entry; to play it they open a folder of their own that holds the same file (a folder of the original footage works as it is, matched by name and size). The browser asks before it opens the folder again each visit. A project that uses such a file cannot be moved to another space.
- Timelines from and for other editors: **Edit → Import timeline…** reads a cut exported from Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or another editor as a Final Cut Pro 7 XML, an EDL or an OpenTimelineIO file, and shows what would come in before anything changes. Each clip finds its file in the library by name; a clip whose file is not there comes in as an empty slot of the right length that names the file it wants. Tracks, cuts, speed, levels, opacity, cross dissolves and markers come across; effects, grades, titles and nested timelines do not. **Edit → Export timeline…** writes the project's video, image and audio clips in any of the three formats, to finish elsewhere and relink by file name.
- Looks: the **Color** section has fourteen ready-made looks (Vivid, Pop, Warm, Golden, Cool, Dusk, Soft, Fade, Vintage, Moody, Drama, B&W, Noir, Sepia). With a look on, **Look strength** turns it down to any share of itself; **None** takes it off.
- Stylized effects: a video or image clip's **Stylize** section has Sharpen, Film grain, Pixelate, Color split, Glitch and VHS, each as how much of it, with **Reset effects**. They are drawn on the graphics card (WebGL 2), the same way in the preview, thumbnails and the export. Grain, glitch and VHS move over time and always look the same at the same moment.
- Color grading: under **Wheels, curves and LUT** in the Color section are three color wheels (Shadows, Midtones, Highlights, each a puck dragged towards a color and a brightness number), a curve editor (one curve for all colors and one each for red, green and blue; a press adds a point, a double click or Delete removes it) and a **LUT** menu. **Import .cube** reads a look-up table file into the project, where every clip can use it, with a **LUT strength**. A table is kept with the project (sampled down to 17 steps a side, about 20 KB), up to 8 of them.
- Animated filters: every color number and stylized effect has a keyframe diamond beside it and a row in the **Effects** tab, so a filter can fade in or change while a clip plays.
- Adjustment layers: **Add adjustment layer** (right-click the timeline, or the top bar's search) puts a clip with no picture on the top video or overlay track. For as long as it lasts it puts its looks, colors, grade and effects on every clip on the tracks under it, and leaves the tracks over it alone. **Amount** is how much of it shows, and can be keyframed to fade a filter in.
- An export stops with a message when a project is graded or stylized and the browser has no graphics card to draw that with, so a video never comes out plainer than its preview.
- Timeline pictures: a video clip on the timeline shows its frames side by side, and an audio clip shows the shape of its sound, both following the clip's trim, speed and the zoom. They are made in your browser from the file the editor plays, one file at a time, so they appear a moment after the clip does. A clip too narrow for them shows its name alone, and a video's sound shows once it is separated onto an audio clip.
- Text looks: in the inspector's **Text** section a text clip can be italic or underlined, set the room between its letters and between its lines, and take an **Outline** (color and width), a **Shadow** (color, opacity, blur and offset) and a **Background** box behind its lines (color, opacity, padding and rounding). Each shows in the preview, in thumbnails and in the export, and AI agents can read and set them as style fields.
- Speed: a video or audio clip's **Speed** in the inspector, from 10% to 1600% of as recorded. The clip's length follows, so twice as fast is half as long, and it never grows into the clip after it. **Reverse** (`Shift+R`) plays a video clip backward, and **Freeze frame** (`Shift+F`) holds the frame under the playhead for two seconds, pushing the rest of the track later. A clip that plays backward or holds a frame has no sound and no captions. The timeline says how each such clip plays. AI agents can read and set a clip's speed, a negative one (backward) and 0 (a held frame) included.
- Captions: the left panel's **Captions** tab has an **Auto captions** button that listens to a video or audio clip and gives it captions, drawn in the preview, in thumbnails and in the export. The speech-to-text runs in your browser, with a speech model that the browser downloads the first time and then keeps. Nothing is sent anywhere to be transcribed. It understands English only for now. The words are saved with the media file, so a teammate who uses the same file gets its captions at once. The captions follow the clip when it is moved, trimmed or split, and when an AI agent changes its speed. Four looks (Block, Karaoke, One word and Bar) share one style (font, size, colors, position, uppercase) for the whole project, and each caption line can be retyped in the **Captions** tab or clicked on the Captions track. **Remove captions** and **Transcribe again** are there too. The limits are in [the reference](reference.md#media-limits). AI agents can read captions, fix words, restyle them, and switch them on for a file that already has saved captions; they cannot start a transcription.
- Post to YouTube, on servers where it is set up: a space's admin connects the space's YouTube channel in space settings (**Socials**), and after an export anyone who can edit there can press **Post to YouTube** in the Export dialog, give the video a title, a description and who can see it (Private to start with), and post it. The video goes from your browser straight to YouTube. Upright videos of three minutes or less show as Shorts. A space can post 10 videos a day.
- Move a project to another of your spaces from its menu (Move to…); its media is copied there, and changes made before the move can no longer be undone.

The editor needs a window at least 1024 px wide. Changes made while offline or signed out wait in the open tab and are sent once you are back. They are not kept if the tab is closed or reloaded first, and the browser asks before that happens. The browser's Back button does not ask, so use **Projects** to leave while changes are waiting. Clicking **Projects** while a change is still being sent waits a moment for it; if it can't be sent, the browser asks before leaving. Closing the tab or clicking **Projects** during an export asks first; the Back button does not, and cancels the export. Without a save dialog (Firefox, Safari), closing the tab or clicking **Projects** also asks while a finished video waits for **Download**. Keep the tab open until the export finishes: on battery, Chrome may pause a tab that is in the background. If the tab is closed, reloaded or crashes during an export, the empty file the save dialog made stays where you saved it.

## What's next

Spaces are live: teams, with a switcher between them, and you can create a team and invite people to it by link (space settings → Members). An invite names an email address and a role (Admin or Editor: everyone in a team can edit); the person opens the link, signs in with that Google account and chooses Join or Decline. Admins see the team's pending invites there, can copy a link again, renew an expired invite or cancel one, and an invite lets its person in even while sign-up is closed. An admin can rename a team (its links stay the same) and delete it once it has no projects. Each team's storage lives in one Google account that holds no other space's storage. Everyone has a Personal space, with its own Google Drive storage, once the server sets `PERSONAL_SPACES` ([Personal spaces](setup.md#personal-spaces)) and its storage settings ([Storage per space](setup.md#storage-per-space)); sign-up is open to anyone with a Google account once the server sets `OPEN_SIGNUP`. Next up: invites sent by email. The [roadmap in the README](../README.md#roadmap) has the stages in order.

After that: transcripts and footage search for Claude, the render station, and posting to Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn.
