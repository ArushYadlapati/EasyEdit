# Project status

What EasyEdit can do in its current release, and what is planned next. For roles, formats and limits, see [reference.md](reference.md).

## What works today

- Sign in with Google. Sign-up is open to anyone with a Google account, and everyone gets a Personal space with its own Google Drive storage, alongside any teams they join. While the server leaves sign-up closed, anyone not on the allowlist or invited to a team is turned away.
- A short welcome for new accounts, on their first sign-in: connect the Google Drive of the account you signed in with, or choose a different Google account, for your Personal space's videos (shown only when Personal spaces and storage are set up; whether it worked or not, you come back to this step and it says so before you move on), then connect an AI agent. Every step can be skipped. Someone who joins a team from an invite sees only the AI agents step, then lands in the team. Accounts that existed before it was added skip it.
- An Admin page where admins add people, change their role, and remove them.
- A dashboard where editors create projects in one of five video formats or at a custom size (in pixels, centimeters or inches), keep them in folders, and rename, duplicate and delete them. Recent shows the projects you opened last. A deleted project is hidden and not shown again. Each card shows a picture of its project, which the editor draws a few seconds after a change is saved; a project changed from outside the editor shows its new picture once an editor opens it.
- The editor, which opens a project in the whole window: an add panel on the left, the preview in the middle, the details on the right and the timeline along the bottom. It is always dark, and it asks the Dark Reader browser extension to leave it alone. In it you can:
  - add text clips from three presets (Title, Subtitle, Lower third) and video, image and audio clips from the Media tab, and move, trim, split, delete, ripple-delete, copy and paste clips of every kind
  - add, rename, lock, hide, mute, reorder and remove tracks
  - play the project in the preview, with frame steps, J, K and L, and safe-zone guides for each format
  - undo and redo your own changes, and undo any change listed under Activity, including a teammate's
- Export: the **Export** button (or `Cmd/Ctrl+E`) makes an MP4 of the project on your own computer, in the browser; nothing is uploaded. You pick a size, watch the progress and can cancel, and you can keep editing while it runs. Video and image clips export from the files the editor plays, so video comes from its 720p preview copy. The sound is mixed as the preview plays it: each video's own sound and the audio tracks, at each clip's volume (up to 1), with muted tracks and hidden video tracks silent; a clip at another speed keeps its pitch. Without an AAC encoder (Firefox, Chrome on Linux) the file has no sound track; when the project has sound, the dialog says so before you export. Viewers can export too. It needs a recent Chrome or Edge on a computer.
- Upload video, images and audio in the editor's Media tab (up to 2 GB a file). Uploads go straight from your browser to the team's Google Shared Drive and resume after an interruption.
- Put media on the timeline, trim it, and play it in the preview. Each video gets a small 720p preview copy, so phone footage plays on any laptop.
- Every change is saved as you make it. Other people's changes show up within about two seconds while you are working, and the top bar says who is editing.
- Viewers get the same editor, read-only: they can play and look, but not change anything.
- A project API (see [The project API](api.md)).
- Each space has a brand kit: fonts for titles, subtitles and body text (about 40 fonts, Orbitron and Inter among them), a color palette and logos. It is in the editor's **Brand kit** tab, where a click adds the text, recolors the selected text or adds the logo, and on the dashboard under the spaces. New text from the Text tab or an AI agent starts in the kit's fonts, and the inspector's **Font** menu lists the kit's fonts first.
- A guide on video: short lessons (the basics, tools and the timeline, text and captions, effects and color, sound, spaces, teams, AI agents, export), each with chapters, captions and a transcript whose lines play from where they are clicked. It is on the **Learn** page (the **Learn** link at the top of the dashboard, **Learn** in the account menu, or **Help** in the editor), with a search of everything said in it, and its first lesson is shown once to each person in a welcome dialog on their first visit to their dashboard. On servers that have the videos ([The guide's videos](setup.md#the-guides-videos)).
- Claude, ChatGPT and other AI agents can list, read, create and edit projects for a signed-in member, through the connector (set up on the AI agents page). When you have more than one space, an agent asks which space a new project goes in. They can also list the team's media and put it on a timeline. A member can connect several agents at once.
- Video skills and your style: agents learn how to make good videos, and how your own videos look and feel, before they edit for you.
  - The `easyedit-video` skill has 14 topics: openings, pacing and cuts, captions, titles and on-screen text, b-roll, music and sound, transitions and motion, color and looks, story shapes, each platform (Shorts, Reels, TikTok and YouTube), editing in someone's style, learning a style, accessibility, and a last check before handing a video back. Each one names EasyEdit's real tools and ops, with numbers and examples.
  - Connected agents have it with nothing to install. The editing guide ends with a list of the topics, `get_editing_guide` with `topic` sends one in full, and each topic is also a prompt (a slash command in Claude Code) and a resource. Claude Code can add it as a plugin with two commands, which brings the connector too. Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI and Copilot unzip `/skills/easyedit-video.zip` (public, no sign-in) into `~/.agents/skills`, and claude.ai takes the same zip as an upload. The AI agents page shows these steps under **Video skills**.
  - Each space has a **Style** tab in its settings, also linked in the sidebar under **Brand kit**. Anyone in the space can read it, and editors and admins change it. It learns from up to 12 videos, links and past projects ([the limits](reference.md#style-limits)).
  - **Add a video** picks one from the space's media, or uploads a new one. EasyEdit looks at it in your browser, one video at a time while the page is open: the shots and how long they are, how fast people talk and their first words, how loud it is, and its overall colors. It saves those numbers and a strip of up to 12 frames. The video itself stays in your storage. The words come from the video's saved captions, or are heard in your browser as Auto captions hears them, and saved for Auto captions too.
  - **Paste a link** takes a YouTube, Vimeo or TikTok video. EasyEdit reads only the video's public title, author and picture (from Vimeo also its length and description), never the video. An Instagram link is turned down kindly, because Instagram doesn't let other apps read posts, with a button to upload the video instead.
  - **Pick past projects** offers the space's 20 most recent projects, and counts the cuts, text, captions, transitions, music, looks and effects in each one.
  - **From your YouTube channel**, on servers where posting is set up, lists the 25 newest public videos of the space's connected channel to add (private and unlisted ones are left out). Their details from YouTube are cut back to the video's address after 30 days, with **Refresh** to read them again, and deleted when the channel is disconnected or another channel takes its place.
  - The style itself is a short form: a sentence or two, where you post, typical length, pace, how you open, captions, on-screen text, music, transitions, look, voice and tone, **Always** and **Never** lists, and notes for AI agents. **Fill in from my videos** fills in only the fields that are still empty and marks them; nothing is kept until **Save**. **Try it** gives the line to ask an agent, with **Copy**. The editor's **Brand kit** tab ends with a **Style** card: the style at a glance and a link to the page.
  - Agents read the style with `get_editing_guide` and the project's `space`: the guide ends with the style and one line of facts for each video, link and project. Topic `"style"` with a space gives it in full, with up to six frame strips as pictures and a text line with each frame's time, for agents that can't show pictures. `get_project` says when a project's space has a style.
  - `save_style` lets an editor's or admin's agent change the summary, the fields and the notes, and add up to five links. Agents are told to save only once the person agrees. Two prompts, **learn-my-style** and **edit-in-my-style**, walk an agent through learning a style and editing in it. The details are in [AI agents](ai-agents.md).
- Effects: the right panel's **Effects** tab, beside Inspect and Activity, holds the selected clip's effects. Video and image clips take a transition in and out (fade, crossfade from the clip before, slide, zoom, whip or dip to a color), with its length and direction. Keyframes animate a clip's position, scale, rotation and opacity, each with its own easing: the diamond buttons beside Inspect's fields add them, and the Effects tab lists every keyframe with its time, value and easing to edit, **Go to** and **Delete** on each and **Clear** for a property, and **Ken Burns** for a slow push in across an image. Text clips animate in and out (fade, pop, slide, and typewriter or scale punch on the way in) and can shake or pulse for the whole clip, with an **Amount** and a **Speed** in percent of the preset's own look (`emphasis.amount`, 0.1 to 3, and `emphasis.speed`, 0.25 to 4, both 1 when absent). The shake is timed in seconds from the clip's start, so it runs at the same rate at any frame rate, up to the fastest a frame can show: past that (a Speed above about 180% at 30 fps, or the vertical jitter at 24 and 25 fps) a wobble is held at that rate rather than looking slower. Highlight word is no longer offered, since it lit the words on a timer rather than as they're said: a clip that already has it keeps it, draws it as before, and its menu still lists it with its color; agents are no longer taught it, and the skills keep emphasis to one line at most. The timeline marks transitions and keyframes on each clip, the export draws them as the preview does, and AI agents can read and set them.
- Templates: a template is a project with slots, the empty places for text, video, images or audio that get filled in later. Each space keeps its own templates, and three starters (Match Recap, Robot Reveal and Season Recap) are there from the start. In the editor, **Make this a slot** in the inspector marks a clip as a slot, and **Save as template** keeps the project as a template of the space (a template with the same name can be replaced). A new project can start from a template on the dashboard, and the **Templates** page in space settings lists them and edits or deletes the saved ones. An empty slot shows as a placeholder; drop a video, image or audio file from Media onto it, or select it and click the file, to fill it. An empty slot exports as a black or silent gap, and the export dialog names the slots still empty. Media that a template keeps cannot be deleted until the template is. AI agents can list and read templates, start a project from one and fill its slots.
- Daily limits keep one person from filling the storage or the database by accident. Over any 24 hours, each person can save 20 MB of changes (at up to 600 changes a minute), create 25 new projects (a duplicate counts as one) and start 500 uploads (resuming an unfinished upload does not count). Each space can hold up to 200 folders. AI agents have their own separate allowance, so an agent does not use up yours. When you reach a limit, the app says so and keeps what you were doing open; nothing already saved is affected, and it saves again once the limit allows.
- Linked clips: `Cmd/Ctrl+G` links the selected clips and `Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+G` unlinks them. A click on a linked clip in the timeline selects its whole group, so one drag moves them all and one Delete removes them all; hold `Alt` to take the one clip, and a click on the preview still selects one. Linked clips show a small chain mark. **Separate audio** leaves the video and its new audio clip linked.
- Export a part: the Export dialog's **What to export** menu exports the whole project, only the span of the selected clips, or from the playhead to the end. A clip cut at the edge of the part keeps the same picture and sound on every frame it still has.
- Export quality: the Export dialog's **Quality** menu gives the picture fewer bits for a smaller file, the standard amount, or more for footage with a lot of motion or detail. The size it may reach is shown before you export.
- Frame rate: a new blank project is made at 24, 25, 30, 50 or 60 frames a second, picked in the New project dialog (30 unless you pick another). A project keeps the rate it was made with, and a project from a template has the template's. The preview, the timeline and the export all run at the project's rate.
- Full quality: the Export dialog has a **Full quality** switch, off unless you turn it on, wherever a video plays from a smaller copy of itself. With it on, each video's picture is drawn from its original file and not the 720p copy the editor plays; the originals are downloaded first, so it is slower. A file this browser can't read, or a high dynamic range one, exports from its copy as before. Sound still comes from the file the editor plays.
- Sound clean-up: a video or audio clip's Sound section has **Clean up voice**, which in one change takes out rumble, turns down the gaps between words, lifts speech where it is understood and brings loud and quiet words closer together; **Reset** takes it off. Each part can be set on its own: **Cut rumble**, **Noise gate**, **Compressor**, and **Bass**, **Presence** and **Treble** in decibels. With several clips selected the button cleans up each one. It is heard in the preview and is in the export, from the same code (`src/lib/audio/clean-dsp.ts`; the preview runs it in an audio worklet, `public/audio/clean-worklet.js`, made by `node scripts/make-worklet.mjs`). In a browser without audio worklets the preview plays clips as recorded; the export is cleaned all the same. It needs no model and no service. Not built: noise reduction that removes hiss under speech, and studio-grade voice repair.
- Limiter: the whole mix is held just under full scale (−1 dB), in the preview and in every export, so a clip turned up past 100% or several loud clips together are turned down smoothly where they used to be clipped flat. A mix that never gets that loud is not changed at all.
- Mixing: selecting a video, overlay or audio track shows its **Track volume**, which scales every clip on it on top of each clip's own volume. A video or audio clip's **Pan** moves its sound towards the left or the right. The preview and the export follow both.
- Voiceover: **Record voiceover** in the Media tab records your microphone while the project plays from the playhead. **Stop recording** saves the take as a sound file, uploads it like any other file, and puts it on an audio track where the recording began. The browser asks for the microphone the first time; wear headphones so the project's own sound is not recorded with your voice. **Record screen** and **Record camera** beside it record a window or tab, or your camera with your microphone, and land the take on a video track the same way.
- Green screen and rounded corners: in a video or image clip's **Color** section, **Green screen** takes one color out of the picture so what is under the clip shows there, with the color, how near it a pixel must be and how softly the edge fades; **Corner rounding** rounds the picture's corners, up to a circle for a square crop. Both show in the preview and the export.
- Subtitle files: the **Captions** tab downloads the project's caption lines as a `.srt` or `.vtt` file, timed on the project's own clock, to post beside the exported video. **Import subtitles** reads a `.srt` or `.vtt` file as the captions of the one selected video or audio clip: each line lands where the clip plays at that moment, and a line's words share its time evenly.
- Trim modes: the button beside **Snap** (or `W`) sets what dragging a clip's edge does. **Trim** is the plain one. **Ripple trim** moves every later clip on the track with the edge, so no gap opens and nothing is covered; at a clip's start the clip stays where it is and gains or loses footage at its head. **Roll** moves the cut between two clips that touch, one growing as the other shrinks. A video or audio clip's **Footage from** field in the inspector slips it: it starts from another moment of its file and keeps its place and length.
- Markers: **B** puts a marker at the playhead, or takes away the one there. Markers show as flags on the timeline's ruler, are kept with the project for everyone, and can be named and removed in the inspector when nothing is selected. `Alt+Up` and `Alt+Down` jump between them, and dragged clips, the Razor and the playhead's jumps stop at them as they do at clip edges.
- Cut by the words: once a clip has captions, the **Captions** tab's lines have a second mode, **Cut video**. Each word is a button; pick words and **Cut words** takes those moments out of the video itself and moves up what follows on the clip's track. **Cut pauses** removes every silence between words longer than 0.6 seconds, leaving a breath either side, and **Cut filler words** removes the ums and uhs. Each is one change and one undo, and says how much shorter the video got. It cuts only the clip's own track, so music or titles on other tracks stay where they are.
- Volume over time: the ◆ beside **Volume** keys a video or audio clip's volume at the playhead, so it can rise and fall across the clip; the keyframes are listed and edited in the Effects tab like any others, and the preview and the export follow them. **Duck under speech** (`Shift+D`, or the button on an audio clip) turns the selected music down to a quarter while other clips are heard over it and back up after; where a file has captions it follows the words, so the music returns in a long pause.
- Color: a video or image clip's **Color** section in the inspector starts with a **Look** menu of ready-made looks, then the numbers behind them: brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, hue, vignette and blur. **Reset color** takes the picture back to as recorded. **Blend** sets how a video, image, text or shape mixes with what is under it (Multiply, Screen, Overlay and others), and flips a video or image sideways or upside down. All of it shows in the preview, in thumbnails and in the export, and AI agents can read and set it. A browser that cannot filter a canvas (older Safari) shows the picture without its colour changes; the export, which needs Chrome or Edge, always has them.
- Reframe: a video clip whose shape is not the frame's (a wide shot in an upright project, or the other way) has a **Reframe** section. **Reframe to follow the action** reads the part of the file the clip plays (small frames, ten looks a second), finds the middle of what moved once the camera's own move is taken out, calms that into a path, and makes the clip fill the frame with position keyframes along the side that does not fit, as one change. It follows movement, not faces: with several things moving it goes to the middle of them, and a still clip is left as it is. Not for a clip that is turned, flipped, backwards, frozen or longer than five minutes.
- Remove background: an image clip's inspector has a **Background** section. **Remove background** runs a portrait matting model (MODNet, Apache 2.0, 26 MB, fetched once from Hugging Face at a pinned revision) in a worker in the browser, makes a PNG with the background see-through, uploads it as a picture of its own ("name (cut out).png") and switches the clip to it as one change; the first picture stays in the library and Undo brings it back. It says how far it is and can be cancelled until the upload starts. It works on people: a picture with no one in it is refused, and nothing is changed. A picture larger than 2048 px on its longer side is brought down to that first. The picture never leaves the browser. Pictures only: a video's background is the green-screen key's job.
- Phone layout: below 1024 px wide the editor no longer asks for a wider window. The same regions stack: the top bar (logo, Edit, View, the name, undo, redo, Settings, Export; the search, the save status and the keyboard list give way), the preview (38% of the height), a switch **Timeline / Add / Details**, and under it whichever is chosen. All three stay mounted, so nothing starts over on a switch and nothing is mounted twice. On the timeline the column of track names is 116 px (240 on a wide window): a name and its options menu, which now has **Lock / Hide / Mute track** at every width. The control row scrolls sideways and the audio meter is not shown. Touch works as the mouse does (tap, drag a clip, scrub the ruler). Play and the timecode are on the Timeline tab only. Fields are 16 px so iOS does not zoom the page. Not checked on a real phone.
- Review: the right panel has a fourth tab, **Review**, with **Comments** and **Versions**. A comment is written at the playhead by anyone who can open the project (a viewer too); it lists by its moment, its timecode takes the playhead there, and each open thread is an amber dot on the ruler. Threads take replies (one level), **Resolve** / **Reopen** (the writer or an editor; resolved threads hide behind *Show resolved*), **Edit** (the writer) and **Delete** (the writer or an admin). The list refreshes every 30 s while the tab is open. A **version** keeps the timeline as the server has it under a name (editors; up to 20 a project; it waits for unsent changes). **Open as a new project** makes a project beside this one with that timeline; a version is never put back over the project. A version saved before the project moved between spaces can't be opened (its media stayed behind). Needs migration `0027_review` (two new tables). Agents have no tools for either yet.
- Stock library: on a server with `PEXELS_API_KEY` set, the Media tab has a **Stock library** button (none without the key). It opens a search of Pexels' free videos and photos: what is popular until something is searched for, Videos or Photos, Show more. **Add** fetches the file in the browser straight from Pexels' file servers (a video at 1080p or the nearest under it) and puts it through the upload queue, so it lands in the space's library like any upload. Only the search words go to Pexels, through `GET /api/stock`; the key stays on the server; Pexels is sent no cookies and no referrer. Not yet set up on the live site (no key).
- Multicam: with videos on two or more video or overlay tracks selected, the inspector has a **Multicam** section. Each track is a camera (top row first). **Cut to N** cuts at the playhead, also while playing: the camera picked shows, and every other is hidden, from the playhead to the next change of camera (or the end of that camera's clip when there is none), so a first cut runs to the end and a cut made between two others changes only that stretch. A hidden stretch is the same clip at opacity 0 (cut where needed), so nothing is lost and each cut is one undo. **Sound from** takes the sound from one camera and silences the others. Line the cameras up first with **Sync by sound**. There is no grid that shows every camera at once, and no number-key switching yet.
- Stabilize: a video clip has a **Stabilize** section (Light, Normal, Strong). It reads the part of the file the clip plays at 192 pixels, measures how the whole picture shifts between each frame and the next (the middle shift of a grid of twelve squares, `shiftBetween`), evens out the camera's path, and keeps on the clip the move that puts each frame on the even path: `steady: { from, zoom, data }`, two 16-bit numbers a file frame in base64 (`src/lib/media/steady.ts`), at most 9,000 frames. The picture is drawn up to 25% larger so the moves never show an edge; the preview and the export draw it the same (`drawMedia`). It moves the picture only: it does not turn or reshape it, so it takes out a hand's shake, not a rolling or wobbling one. Where a third or more of the frames have too little in them to read (flat colour, dark), it changes nothing and says so. **Remove stabilizing** takes it off.
- Motion tracking (Follow): a text, shape or image clip has a **Follow** section. Put the clip on something in the video under it at the playhead and press **Follow what is under it**: the thing is followed through the video's frames, forward and backward, for as long as the clip and the video both last (up to 30 seconds), and the clip gets x and y keyframes that keep it there (at most 100, the path evened out and thinned; they are ordinary keyframes in the Effects tab). It reads the file the preview plays at 256 pixels and matches a small square from frame to frame (`src/lib/media/motion.ts`); it stops where the thing is lost and says after how long. The video under it must not be rotated, flipped or moved by keyframes. It follows a point only: the clip does not grow or turn with the thing.
- Nesting: several selected clips become one clip, a **nest** (right-click **Nest clips**, or the **Nest** section when several clips are selected; **Unnest** takes one apart). A nest (`type: "nest"`) holds a small timeline of its own (`tracks`, starting at frame 0; every kind of clip but an empty slot and another nest) and plays `duration` frames of it from `in`. It moves, trims, splits and copies as one clip. On the frame it is one picture: its tracks are drawn together on a layer (`drawNest`), and that layer is placed, scaled, faded, blended, coloured, graded and masked as a whole, with transitions and keyframes. Its sound is its clips' sound times its own **Volume**. Readers that find media, sound, fonts and files in use see the project with nests laid out as ordinary clips where they play (`expandNests`, clips named `<nest id>/<clip id>`), so the preview, the export and the server's checks need nothing more. What a nest holds is set when it is made (`tracks` cannot be patched): to change it, unnest, edit, nest again. Limits: captions of clips inside a nest are not drawn; a nest takes no crop; a project with a nest cannot be moved between spaces until it is unnested; unnesting drops what was set on the nest itself.
- Mixed text styles: some of a text clip's words can be **bold**, *italic*, underlined or another colour while the rest keeps the clip's look. Select words in the inspector's Text box and use **Selected words** (Bold, Italic, Underline, Plain, a colour). They are kept as `spans` on the clip (`{from, to, color?, fontWeight?, italic?, underline?}`, at most 40, in order, not overlapping; offsets count as a text field counts) and hold only what differs from the clip's style. When the words are changed the spans move with them (`remapSpans`: typing inside a span extends it, a span swallowed by the change goes), and an undo puts both back. A text with spans is wrapped and drawn stretch by stretch, each measured in its own font, through the same outline, shadow, typewriter and highlight-word passes; a text with none is drawn exactly as before. The export asks for each weight a span uses. Font and size cannot differ inside one clip.
- Source monitor: a library file's menu has **Open in source monitor**. It plays and scrubs that one file on its own, with **Mark In** and **Mark Out** (playing stops at the Out), and puts the marked part on a track at the timeline's playhead: **Insert** cuts a clip the playhead is inside and moves everything from there on later on that track; **Overwrite** clears what is under it (trimming, removing or cutting a clip in two around it). The playhead then moves to the new clip's end and the monitor stays open, so parts can be laid one after another. Keys while it is open: I, O, Space, the arrows, comma (insert), full stop (overwrite). A **Track** menu picks among the unlocked tracks that take the file; with none, a new track is made. An image has a length in place of marks. Insert moves only its own track.
- Noise reduction: a clip's **Clean up** has **Noise reduction** in percent, and **Clean up voice** now sets it to 40%. It turns down the steady noise under a recording (hiss, a fan, a hum). The sound is split into 257 pitches in short overlapping pieces; at each pitch the quietest the sound has been in the last two seconds is taken for the noise, and a pitch no louder than its noise is turned down (to −6 dB at the lightest, −30 dB at the strongest). It is our own code in `src/lib/audio/clean-dsp.ts` (`createDenoiser`), so the preview's worklet and the export run the same thing; it is not RNNoise. It delays the clip's sound by 512 samples (about a hundredth of a second), comes in over the first 0.6 s of a clip so a first word is not taken for noise, and takes a note held for more than two seconds for noise, so it is for a voice, not music. After changing it run `node scripts/make-worklet.mjs`.
- Captions in other languages: the Captions tab has **Spoken language** (46 languages, English first) and, for any language but English, **Translate to English**. The speech model was already the multilingual one; the language was fixed to English. Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Khmer, Lao and Burmese are captioned in English only, because caption lines are built, wrapped, lit and retyped word by word with spaces between. A right-to-left line (Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, Urdu) is drawn whole, so Karaoke does not light its words one by one. The choice is remembered in the browser (`easyedit:speech`), not in the project. Words saved on the server are marked with what they were heard as (`speechTag`: the model name, then `#spanish` or `#spanish>english`; English as spoken keeps the bare model name, so older rows still match, and no migration was needed), and only words with the wanted mark are reused. Clips that already have captions keep them until **Transcribe again**.
- Sync by sound: with two or more video or audio clips selected, **Sync by sound** (in the Sound section) listens to each and moves all but one so their sound lines up, for a camera and a separate microphone or two cameras. The first video stays where it is. It needs at least four seconds in common, and the less the recordings overlap the more alike they have to be; otherwise it says it could not find the same sound and moves nothing. A clip that belongs before the timeline's start keeps the part that fits.
- Scene detection: a video clip that plays forward has a **Scenes** section with **Split at scene changes**. It looks through the part of the file the clip plays (every frame, small), finds where the shot changes, and splits the clip there as one change. A cut is a frame unlike the one before it, far more than the frames around it differ, so motion and zooms are not cuts.
- Export formats: the Export dialog has a **Format** menu. **MP4 video** (H.264, AAC) as before; **WebM video** (VP9, Opus), which also works in a browser built without H.264; **Animated GIF** (fits inside 480 pixels a side, about 15 frames a second, no sound, loops; written by our own code in `src/lib/export/gif.ts`, each frame with its own 255 colours and unchanged pixels left see-through); **Sound only** as M4A (AAC) or WAV (the mix itself, 16 bits). A GIF and sound alone have no Size or Quality to pick. Only an MP4 is offered for posting to YouTube. ProRes is not offered: a browser cannot encode it.
- Speed ramps: the **Speed** section of a video clip that plays forward has **Speed ramp** (Speed up, Slow down, "Fast, slow, fast", Slow-motion moment, Fast start, Fast finish) and **Apply ramp**. The clip becomes up to ten short linked pieces, each a little faster or slower than the one before, playing its footage end to end; what follows on the track moves to fit. One undo puts the clip back. An audio clip has a speed but no ramp in the inspector.
- Scopes: in the **Color** section, **Scopes** shows the frame in the preview as a histogram, a waveform or a vectorscope, redrawn as the preview is (while a wheel or a curve is dragged too). They are counted only while shown.
- Masks: a video, image or adjustment layer has a **Mask** section in the inspector. Switched on, only a rectangle or ellipse of the picture shows (or, with **Invert**, everything but it), placed and sized in percent of the picture, with a **Soft edge**. On an adjustment layer the mask is the part of the frame the layer changes, which is how to blur, grey or pixelate one region. It is drawn the same in the preview, thumbnails and the export.
- Slide: the trim-mode button in the control row (and `W`) now steps through four modes. With **Slide** on, dragging a clip that touches the clips beside it moves it between them: the one before ends later or sooner and the one after starts later or sooner, so the clip keeps its footage and length and nothing else on the track moves. It stops where a neighbour would run out of footage or of frames.
- Keyframe curves and crop keyframes: in the **Effects** tab a keyframe's Ease menu has **Custom curve**, which shows the travel to the next keyframe as a curve with two handles to drag (or nudge with the arrow keys); a handle pulled past the top or bottom overshoots and comes back. Each side of a clip's crop has a keyframe diamond in the inspector, so a crop can open, close or wipe across over time; **Reset crop** clears them.
- Media in a folder on your computer, in your Personal space only: in the **Media** tab, **Use a folder on this computer** asks for a folder (Chrome and Edge). From then on, files you add are copied into that folder and not sent to Google Drive, with the preview copy and still picture made from each beside them, and the editor plays them from there. It works in a Personal space that has no storage connected at all. The library still lists every file, so your other devices see the entry; to play it there, open a folder that holds the same file (a folder of the original footage works as it is, matched by name and size). The browser asks before it opens the folder again each visit. A project that uses such a file cannot be moved to another space. A team is not offered a folder: its media goes to its Google Drive, where every member can play it.
- Timelines from and for other editors: **Edit → Import timeline…** reads a cut exported from Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or another editor as a Final Cut Pro 7 XML, an EDL or an OpenTimelineIO file, and shows what would come in before anything changes. Each clip finds its file in the library by name; a clip whose file is not there comes in as an empty slot of the right length that names the file it wants. Tracks, cuts, speed, levels, opacity, cross dissolves and markers come across; effects, grades, titles and nested timelines do not. **Edit → Export timeline…** writes the project's video, image and audio clips in any of the three formats, to finish elsewhere and relink by file name.
- Looks: the **Color** section's **Look** menu, at the top above the numbers a look sets, has ten ready-made looks (Vivid, Warm, Cool, Dusk, Fade, Vintage, Moody, Drama, B&W, Sepia). With a look on, **Look strength** beside it turns it down to any share of itself; **None** takes it off. Numbers that match no look show as **Custom**, which is in the menu only then. Pop, Golden, Soft and Noir were taken out as near copies of Vivid, Warm, Fade and B&W: a clip that had one keeps its colours and shows as Custom, and a space's Style (or a past project's counted looks) that names one reads as the kept look nearest it.
- Stylized effects: a video or image clip's **Stylize** section has Sharpen, Film grain and Pixelate, and under **More effects** Color split, Glitch and VHS (open by itself while the clip has one of those on or keyframed), each as how much of it, with **Reset effects**. They are drawn on the graphics card (WebGL 2), the same way in the preview, thumbnails and the export. Grain, glitch and VHS move over time and always look the same at the same moment.
- Color grading: under **Wheels, curves and LUT** in the Color section are three color wheels (Shadows, Midtones, Highlights, each a puck dragged towards a color and a brightness number), a curve editor (one curve for all colors and one each for red, green and blue; a press adds a point, a double click or Delete removes it) and a **LUT** menu. **Import .cube** reads a look-up table file into the project, where every clip can use it, with a **LUT strength**. A table is kept with the project (sampled down to 17 steps a side, about 20 KB), up to 8 of them.
- Animated filters: every color number and stylized effect has a keyframe diamond beside it and a row in the **Effects** tab, so a filter can fade in or change while a clip plays.
- Adjustment layers: **Add adjustment layer** (right-click the timeline, or the top bar's search) puts a clip with no picture on the top video or overlay track. For as long as it lasts it puts its looks, colors, grade and effects on every clip on the tracks under it, and leaves the tracks over it alone. **Amount** is how much of it shows, and can be keyframed to fade a filter in.
- An export stops with a message when a project is graded or stylized and the browser has no graphics card to draw that with, so a video never comes out plainer than its preview.
- Timeline pictures: a video clip on the timeline shows its frames side by side, and an audio clip shows the shape of its sound, both following the clip's trim, speed and the zoom. They are made in your browser from the file the editor plays, one file at a time, so they appear a moment after the clip does. A clip too narrow for them shows its name alone, and a video's sound shows once it is separated onto an audio clip.
- Text looks: in the inspector's **Text** section a text clip can be italic or underlined, set the room between its letters and between its lines, and take an **Outline** (color and width), a **Shadow** (color, opacity, blur and offset) and a **Background** box behind its lines (color, opacity, padding and rounding). Each shows in the preview, in thumbnails and in the export, and AI agents can read and set them as style fields.
- Speed: a video or audio clip's **Speed** in the inspector, from 10% to 1600% of as recorded. The clip's length follows, so twice as fast is half as long, and it never grows into the clip after it. **Reverse** (`Shift+R`) plays a video clip backward, and **Freeze frame** (`Shift+F`) holds the frame under the playhead for two seconds, pushing the rest of the track later. A clip that plays backward or holds a frame has no sound and no captions. The timeline says how each such clip plays. AI agents can read and set a clip's speed, a negative one (backward) and 0 (a held frame) included.
- Captions: the left panel's **Captions** tab has an **Auto captions** button that listens to a video or audio clip and gives it captions, drawn in the preview, in thumbnails and in the export. The speech-to-text runs in your browser, with a speech model that the browser downloads the first time and then keeps. Nothing is sent anywhere to be transcribed. It understands English only for now. The words are saved with the media file, so a teammate who uses the same file gets its captions at once. The captions follow the clip when it is moved, trimmed or split, and when an AI agent changes its speed. Four looks (Block, Karaoke, One word and Bar; a new project starts on Karaoke when it is upright or square, and on Block when it is wide) share one style (font, size, colors, position, uppercase) for the whole project, and each caption line can be retyped in the **Captions** tab or clicked on the Captions track. **Remove captions** and **Transcribe again** are there too. The limits are in [the reference](reference.md#media-limits). AI agents can read captions, fix words, restyle them, and switch them on for a file that already has saved captions; they cannot start a transcription.
- Post to YouTube, on servers where it is set up: a space's admin connects the space's YouTube channel in space settings (**Socials**), and after an export anyone who can edit there can press **Post to YouTube** in the Export dialog, give the video a title, a description and who can see it (Private to start with), and post it. The video goes from your browser straight to YouTube. Upright videos of three minutes or less show as Shorts. A space can post 10 videos a day.
- Move a project to another of your spaces from its menu (Move to…); its media is copied there, and changes made before the move can no longer be undone.

The editor needs a window at least 1024 px wide. Changes made while offline or signed out wait in the open tab and are sent once you are back. They are not kept if the tab is closed or reloaded first, and the browser asks before that happens. The browser's Back button does not ask, so use **Projects** to leave while changes are waiting. Clicking **Projects** while a change is still being sent waits a moment for it; if it can't be sent, the browser asks before leaving. Closing the tab or clicking **Projects** during an export asks first; the Back button does not, and cancels the export. Without a save dialog (Firefox, Safari), closing the tab or clicking **Projects** also asks while a finished video waits for **Download**. Keep the tab open until the export finishes: on battery, Chrome may pause a tab that is in the background. If the tab is closed, reloaded or crashes during an export, the empty file the save dialog made stays where you saved it.

## What's next

Spaces are live: teams, with a switcher between them, and you can create a team and invite people to it by link (space settings → Members). An invite names an email address and a role (Admin or Editor: everyone in a team can edit); the person opens the link, signs in with that Google account and chooses Join or Decline. Admins see the team's pending invites there, can copy a link again, renew an expired invite or cancel one, and an invite lets its person in even while sign-up is closed. An admin can rename a team (its links stay the same) and delete it once it has no projects. Each team's storage lives in one Google account that holds no other space's storage. Everyone has a Personal space, with its own Google Drive storage, once the server sets `PERSONAL_SPACES` ([Personal spaces](setup.md#personal-spaces)) and its storage settings ([Storage per space](setup.md#storage-per-space)); sign-up is open to anyone with a Google account once the server sets `OPEN_SIGNUP`. Next up: invites sent by email. The [roadmap in the README](../README.md#roadmap) has the stages in order.

After that: transcripts and footage search for Claude, the render station, and posting to Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn.

Video skills and the Style page still need a signed-in check on the live site, with real footage and a real agent ([what to click](manual-checks.md#what-to-click-to-check-the-style-page)). Instagram links stay turned down until EasyEdit has an app that Meta approves to read posts; until then, people upload their own copy.
